What a standard page actually weighs

By A. Reviewer · 22 September 2026 · 7 min read

The average article page now ships more bytes of advertising and measurement code than it does words. Most of it arrives before the first paragraph has finished rendering.

Open the network panel on almost any publication and the shape is the same. A handful of requests fetch the text, the images and the stylesheet. Several dozen more fetch bidding scripts, identity syncs and measurement pixels, each one negotiating on your behalf in an auction you were never shown.

None of this is a secret, and none of it is especially controversial among the people who build it. What is striking is how much of the weight is spent on the auction rather than on the advertisement that eventually wins it.

The practical consequence is felt on slow connections and older hardware, where the text you came for waits behind code that exists to describe you to a bidder.

A reasonable response is not to demand that publishing be free, but to insist that the reader's device is not the cheapest place to put the machinery.
